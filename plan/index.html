01

Finish NumPy

5 sessions · the introduction is done, the API is not. Every Pandas operation later is NumPy underneath, so this is not a module to compress.

N1Creating arrays array(), arange, linspace, zeros, ones, eye. dtype and the single-type rule. The list-versus-array contrast: a list repeats, an array multiplies.
N2Shape, dimensions and reshaping shape, ndim, size. reshape with the element-count law, -1 inference, flatten and transpose.
N3Indexing and slicing 1-D and 2-D indexing, row and column slices, negative indices, fancy indexing.
N4Aggregation and broadcasting sum, mean, median, std, min, max. axis=0 goes down, axis=1 goes across — the most-missed idea in the module. Broadcasting rules.
N5Boolean masking and sorting Filtering by condition, combining masks with & | ~, np.where, sort, vstack and hstack. This is the direct ancestor of Pandas filtering.
02

Pandas, end to end

7 sessions · mirrors the sequence June B3 already completed, so the two batches arrive at the merge point holding the same vocabulary.

P1Series and DataFrame Why Pandas sits on NumPy. A Series as a labelled array; a DataFrame as Series sharing an index. Building one from the dictionary-of-lists they already know.
P2Loading files and the first look read_csv and read_excel, then the fixed ritual — head, shape, info, describe. Reading missing counts straight off info().
P3Selection: loc and iloc Column selection, single versus double brackets, label-based against position-based, and why loc includes the endpoint while iloc does not.
P4Filtering and sorting Boolean filtering with & and |, brackets around every condition, isin, between, query. sort_values with multiple keys and directions.
P5Missing values isnull().sum(), dropna with subset and thresh, fillna with mean, median, mode and forward fill — and the judgement call behind each.
P6Duplicates and derived columns duplicated and drop_duplicates with subset and keep. Vectorised new columns, np.where, pd.cut, and apply as the fallback rather than the default.
P7Merge, concat and groupby The common-column rule, the four join types, concat for stacking, then split–apply–combine with agg. Bridge from SQL joins as B3 had it.

The merged syllabus

Seven modules in dependency order. Statistics and probability come first because visualisation, Power BI and Tableau all assume them — a box plot means nothing to a student who has not met a quartile.

01

Statistics

8 sessions · the vocabulary every later module borrows. Taught with Pandas in hand, so every measure is computed on a real column the same day it is defined.

S1Why statistics, and types of data Population versus sample. Nominal, ordinal, interval and ratio — and why the type decides which chart and which test are legal later.
S2Central tendency Mean, median, mode. When each lies. Ties back to the fillna decision they already made in Pandas.
S3Dispersion Range, variance, standard deviation, IQR. Two datasets with the same mean and different spread — the demo that makes the module land.
S4Shape of a distribution Skewness and kurtosis, the normal curve, the empirical rule. Reading shape from describe() before any chart exists.
S5Correlation and covariance Pearson and Spearman, the interpretation bands, and the discipline of not upgrading correlation into causation.
S6Sampling and the central limit theorem Random, stratified and systematic sampling. Sampling distribution, standard error, and why the CLT is what makes inference possible.
S7Confidence intervals Point estimate against interval estimate, confidence level, margin of error. Reporting a range rather than a false precision.
S8Hypothesis testing Null and alternative, significance, p-value, t-test and chi-square. Framed as A/B testing, which is the version they will be interviewed on.
02

Probability

6 sessions · kept tight and applied. Enough to support inference and interviews, without turning into a mathematics course.

Pr1Foundations Experiment, sample space, event. Classical, empirical and subjective probability. The 0-to-1 scale.
Pr2The rules Addition and multiplication rules, mutually exclusive versus independent — the distinction students reliably collapse.
Pr3Conditional probability and Bayes Updating a belief on evidence. The medical-test example, worked slowly, because the intuition is genuinely counter-intuitive.
Pr4Random variables and expected value Discrete against continuous, probability distributions, expectation as a long-run average.
Pr5Binomial and Poisson The two discrete distributions an analyst actually meets, with the conditions that make each applicable.
Pr6The normal distribution and z-scores Standardisation, reading the table, and the link forward to confidence intervals and outlier rules.
03

Data visualisation

10 sessions · matplotlib first for control, seaborn second for speed. Every chart plots a groupby result both batches already know how to produce.

V1Why charts exist The chart-choice rule: comparison to bar, trend to line, distribution to histogram, relationship to scatter. First plot straight off a groupby.
V2matplotlib fundamentals Figure against axes, figsize, titles, labels, legend, grid, savefig. Labelling made non-negotiable from here on.
V3Bar, line and pie Sorted bars, horizontal bars for long labels, grouped and stacked, multi-line trends, and the honest case for and against pie.
V4Distributions Histograms and the bin-count choice, box plots and the five-number summary, IQR outliers. Pays off S2 and S3 as a picture.
V5Relationships Scatter plots, colour and size encoding, corr() and the matrix. Reading direction by eye before computing the number.
V6seaborn statistical plots The one pattern — data, x, y, hue. countplot, barplot, boxplot, violinplot, histplot. Note that barplot shows the mean, not the total.
V7Heatmaps and multi-panel figures Correlation heatmaps with annot and centre, pivot to heatmap, subplot grids with ax=, tight_layout, pairplot.
V8Time series charts to_datetime first, datetime index, resample, rolling averages, readable date axes. The dtype trap demonstrated before it is explained.
V9Charts that do not lie Truncated axes, chart junk, colour with meaning, colourblind-safe palettes, and titles that state the finding rather than name the columns.
V10Dashboard workshop Four-panel figure on one dataset, written findings under each chart, exported as a single image.
04

Excel

6 sessions · deliberately placed after Python, not before. Students who already know groupby learn pivot tables in one session instead of three — and every analyst job description still asks for Excel.

E1Interface and cell references Workbook structure, entry and formatting, and relative against absolute references. The dollar sign is the concept, not the keystroke.
E2Formulas and logic SUM, AVERAGE, COUNT, COUNTA, ROUND. IF, nested IF, IFS, AND, OR. COUNTIF and SUMIF as the first taste of conditional aggregation.
E3Lookup functions VLOOKUP and its limits, INDEX with MATCH, XLOOKUP where available. Framed directly as the Excel version of a Pandas merge.
E4Cleaning tools Sort, filter, remove duplicates, text-to-columns, TRIM and the text functions, data validation, conditional formatting.
E5Pivot tables Rows, columns, values, filters. Slicers, grouping, calculated fields, pivot charts. Taught as groupby with a mouse.
E6Charts, dashboards and Power Query Native charts, a simple dashboard sheet, what-if and goal seek, then a first look at Power Query — which is the on-ramp to Power BI.
05

Power BI

8 sessions · the heaviest tool module, and the one that most often decides an interview. Power Query lands easily straight after E6.

B1The ecosystem Desktop, Service and Mobile. Install, tour the panes, and the report–dataset–dashboard distinction that confuses everyone at first.
B2Get data and transform Connectors, the Power Query editor, applied steps, type changes, split and merge columns, unpivot. The steps list as an audit trail.
B3Data modelling Relationships, cardinality, filter direction, fact and dimension tables, the star schema. Why one flat sheet stops working.
B4DAX foundations Calculated columns against measures — the single most important distinction in the module. SUM, AVERAGE, COUNTROWS, DISTINCTCOUNT.
B5DAX in context CALCULATE and FILTER, row against filter context, a date table, and time intelligence — year-to-date, prior year, running totals.
B6Visuals Bar, line, card, KPI, matrix, map, gauge. Conditional formatting, and applying the V9 honesty rules inside a BI tool.
B7Interactivity Slicers, cross-filtering, drill-down and drill-through, bookmarks, custom tooltips, buttons and navigation.
B8Publish and share Workspaces, publishing, dashboards from report tiles, scheduled refresh, row-level security, and export options.
06

Tableau

6 sessions · shorter than Power BI because the concepts transfer. Teach the differences, not the basics a second time.

T1Interface and data connection Desktop and Public, live against extract, the data pane, and dimensions against measures — Tableau's central idea.
T2Building views Rows and columns shelves, the Marks card, Show Me, sorting, filtering, and the continuous-versus-discrete blue and green distinction.
T3Calculations Calculated fields, aggregate against row-level, quick table calculations, and a first controlled look at level-of-detail expressions.
T4Parameters, sets and groups User-driven controls, hierarchies and drill paths, bins, and top-N sets.
T5Dashboards Layout containers, dashboard actions — filter, highlight, URL — device layouts, and formatting for a real screen.
T6Stories and publishing Story points for narrative, publishing to Tableau Public as a portfolio piece, and an honest Power BI comparison for interviews.
07

Projects

10 sessions · four builds across the whole stack, ending in presentations and a portfolio each student can send to a recruiter.

PR1Framing a project Turning a vague ask into an answerable question. Dataset selection, success criteria, scope control, and how to present work to a non-technical room.
PR2–3Project one — sales analysis in Python End-to-end EDA and visualisation. Build, then a review session where every student defends one chart.
PR4–5Project two — HR analytics with statistics Attrition drivers using grouped comparisons, correlation and a hypothesis test. The module where statistics stops being theory.
PR6–7Project three — Power BI dashboard Raw source through Power Query, a modelled star schema, DAX measures and an interactive report. Published and reviewed.
PR8–9Capstone — Tableau story Free choice of domain, unseen data, full pipeline. Build plus a rehearsal session with feedback before the final.
PR10Final presentations Two minutes each, chart and finding only, no code on screen. Then portfolio packaging — GitHub, Tableau Public, and the resume lines each project earns.

Running two batches as one room

Before the first joint session

Level the vocabulary

The two batches learned the same Pandas from different mouths and different examples. Open the merged block with one recap session on a shared dataset so the terms, the column names and the shortcuts match.

Mixed experience

Pair, do not stream

Freshers and experienced candidates in one room works if you pair them deliberately in labs. Streaming by ability in a merged batch re-creates the gap you just closed.

Assessment

Checkpoint at each module end

A short applied task after Statistics, Visualisation and Power BI. It catches drift early, and the outputs become portfolio material rather than disposable exam answers.

Datasets

Reuse one, hold one back

Keep a single sales or HR file running across statistics, visualisation and Power BI so students think about the method, not the columns. Reserve genuinely unseen data for the capstone.

Tooling

Check licences early

Power BI Desktop is Windows-only, and Tableau needs either a student licence or Public. Sort access during the Excel module, not on the morning of B1.

If time runs short

Protect projects

Compress Tableau to four sessions and Excel to five before touching the project block. Placement conversations are built on what students can show, not on what was covered.
